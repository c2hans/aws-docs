# Benefits of Matter certification for device makers
<a name="manufacturer-benefits"></a>

In addition to helping consumers, Matter certification provides benefits to smart device manufacturers. By adopting the Matter standard, organizations can reduce costs and expand their customer reach. Since Matter's launch in 2022 and its evolution through version 1.5 (November 2025), manufacturers across the smart-home industry have realized these benefits.

## Single certification across ecosystems
<a name="single-certification-across-ecosystems"></a>

To support compatibility across smart-home systems such as Alexa, HomeKit, Google Home, and Samsung SmartThings, manufacturers complete multiple certification processes with each organization. Matter simplifies this by establishing a single common certification.

Device makers certify their products once to the Matter standard to be compatible with major smart-home systems and voice assistants. This streamlines development and can reduce certification costs compared to maintaining separate certifications. You no longer need to maintain separate certifications as you update products. A single Matter certification can also help support compatibility as new smart-home systems emerge.

The Connectivity Standards Alliance (CSA) has also improved the certification process with tools including the Certification Tool, the Protocol Implementation Conformance Statement (PICS) Tool, and the Zigbee Unified Test Harness (ZUTH), along with expanded testing provider networks and an Interoperability Testing Facility.

## Reduced development costs
<a name="reduced-development-costs"></a>

Matter also helps lower development costs for manufacturers. By adopting a common connectivity and security standard, organizations benefit from shared infrastructure components that contribute to the overall Matter project.

For example, manufacturers no longer need to include their own proprietary Thread border routers in products, because hub manufacturers take on this responsibility. Shared, open source drivers and libraries further reduce redundant engineering work. Common service discovery and device-setup mechanisms reduce the need for custom app development. Manufacturers can pass these reductions in infrastructure and app development costs on to consumers through more affordable smart-home devices.

The Matter SDK has matured since the initial release, and development tools, libraries, and documentation are now available. Matter version 1.4.2 (June 2025) introduced major improvements to transport reliability, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) commissioning, Platform Security Architecture (PSA)-based cryptography, and testing infrastructure. These improvements have reduced integration complexity and can shorten time to market for new products.

As of Matter version 1.5, the standard supports over 50 device types, including lighting, locks, thermostats, appliances (refrigerators, dishwashers, ovens, microwaves), robotic vacuums, energy management devices (solar panels, batteries, heat pumps), electric vehicle chargers, water management devices, air quality sensors, and cameras with streaming support.

## Simplified customer support
<a name="simplified-customer-support"></a>

The current fragmentation in the smart-home market leads to high customer support burdens for manufacturers. Consumers frequently encounter issues with connectivity, setup, and compatibility that require troubleshooting. Matter aims to reduce these problems by standardizing core functions.

When issues do occur, the common underlying Matter protocols mean companies can more easily diagnose and resolve connectivity problems without having to consider multiple proprietary standards. This streamlines the support process. With a single app and common voice compatibility, customers also have an easier time learning to use devices, reducing the need for support in many cases. The simplified customer experience and troubleshooting that Matter provides help reduce long-term support costs for manufacturers.
