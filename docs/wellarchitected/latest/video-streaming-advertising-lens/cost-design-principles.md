# Cost optimization design principles
<a name="cost-design-principles"></a>

 These design principles help you achieve and maintain cost-efficient advertising workloads in the cloud. They extend the [lens-wide design principles](design-principles.md) with guidance specific to maintaining profitable campaigns through a system that accurately tracks campaign costs against value.
+  **Maintain profitable campaigns through complete cost tracking:** Cost optimization for advertising workloads depends on keeping campaigns profitable through a system that accurately tracks campaign costs against value. Focus optimization efforts on minimizing unnecessary infrastructure costs and providing sufficient but not excessive capacity through intelligent auto scaling. Implement effective anti-fraud solutions to reduce costs and process and store data only from valid consumers, customers, and partners.
+  **Minimize unnecessary traffic costs:** A large percentage of bid requests will never result in a win because of mismatched targeting, exhausted budgets, or low-value inventory. Every request processed consumes compute, memory, and network resources with no return. Filter invalid or non-matching bid requests at the network layer before they reach application compute. This reduces both the compute cost and application-level processing latency for traffic that would have been discarded anyway.
+  **Apply rate limiting to control inbound request volume per partner:** Without rate limiting, a single partner's traffic spike can drive unplanned compute scaling and cost overruns. Rate limiting caps inbound QPS per connection, providing cost predictability and protecting downstream resources. Set per-partner QPS limits based on historical traffic analysis and contractual agreements. Review and adjust limits monthly based on partner traffic trends and business value per partner.
+  **Use purpose-built, transaction-based connectivity for partner traffic:** Per-GB data processing gateways such as NAT Gateways charge for every byte transferred plus hourly fees, which compounds quickly at RTB scale. Transaction-based connectivity models align pricing with RTB economics, charging per request rather than per byte, and can reduce networking costs. Evaluate whether routing partner bid traffic through purpose-built connectivity avoids the fundamental pricing model mismatch between per-GB charges and per-transaction advertising value.
+  **Align capacity model to traffic pattern:** Provisioned capacity can provide the lowest cost per hour. However, for unpredictable workloads, dynamic allocation provides a lower overall cost of ownership. Distinguish between components with predictable, consistent traffic (bid evaluation fleets during business hours, which suit provisioned capacity) and components with unpredictable or bursty traffic (campaign launch processing, which suits on-demand or serverless capacity). Apply Spot Instances for interruptible batch workloads where 60–90% cost savings are achievable.
+  **Colocate bidder, database, and connectivity infrastructure to avoid cross-AZ charges:** Cross-AZ data transfer costs ($0.01/GB per direction) accumulate rapidly at RTB scale. Co-locating SSP and DSP components, bidder nodes, and database nodes within the same Availability Zone avoids cross-AZ transfer charges for the high-frequency queries that characterize bid evaluation. Zone-aware load balancing and Availability Zone affinity configuration at the infrastructure level minimize these charges without requiring application code changes.
+  **Measure cost per unit of business value, not aggregate spend:** Track cost-to-revenue ratios per campaign and per workload component rather than only monitoring total AWS spend. Set KPIs for billing metrics (resource costs) and campaign metrics (click-through rate, cost per acquisition). Continually re-evaluate campaigns and adjust resource allocation to meet value generation goals. Visualize cost-to-performance trade-offs in dashboards accessible to both technical and business stakeholders.
