# Sustainability design principles
<a name="sus-design-principles"></a>

These design principles help you minimize the environmental impact of advertising workloads in the cloud. They extend the [lens-wide design principles](design-principles.md) with guidance specific to reducing the energy and resource consumption required to process each bid, deliver each impression, and serve each ad creative.
+  **Set sustainability goals internally and with advertising partners:** Establish and communicate sustainability targets that support business objectives. Work with advertising partners, including SSPs, DSPs, measurement vendors, and publishers, to define shared sustainability expectations. Sustainability progress should be measured and reported using the AWS Sustainability service (formerly the AWS Customer Carbon Footprint Tool) alongside traditional operational KPIs.
+  **Measure energy through resource proxies, and treat account-level carbon reporting as confirmation:** No AWS service reports energy or carbon at the granularity of an individual bid request, impression, agent invocation, stored object, or network path. The best practices in this pillar therefore use the resource signals you can observe directly, such as compute utilization, instance-hours, invocation counts, bytes transferred, and stored bytes, as engineering proxies for energy, and treat the figures derived from them as estimates rather than measurements. The AWS Sustainability service reports estimated emissions at the account and Region level on a monthly cadence with a reporting lag, so it confirms the direction of a trend over time but cannot attribute a change to a single control, threshold, or routing decision. Act on the resource proxies, and read the account-level footprint as lagging confirmation.
+  **Implement low-latency workloads only for time-critical business requirements:** Not every advertising computation requires millisecond response times. Batch workloads for audience segmentation, attribution modeling, campaign reporting, and privacy-enhanced collaboration don't need to run synchronously in the bid evaluation path. Schedule these computations during periods of lower energy demand or higher renewable energy availability. Run real-time bidding infrastructure at the required scale, and run everything else as efficiently scheduled batch jobs.
+  **Use serverless computing, containerization, and cloud-based technologies to scale resources dynamically:** Serverless and containerized advertising workloads automatically scale to zero or near-zero during off-peak periods, reducing idle resource energy consumption. Traditional VM-based deployments run continuously regardless of actual traffic, while containerized workloads scale in response to actual bid volume. This alignment between resource consumption and advertising activity directly reduces energy waste.
+  **Region selection is a complex factor for implementing advertising workloads:** Latency requirements for real-time bidding typically require ad-serving architectures to operate near consuming workloads and publishers. However, advertising analytics workloads such as attribution modeling, audience segmentation, and campaign reporting can be consolidated into fewer AWS regions that use lower-carbon energy. Use the most sustainable AWS region available that meets your latency and data residency requirements for each workload category.
+  **Back up and archive data only when challenging to recreate:** Advertising data has a natural lifecycle, in which bid events, impression logs, and audience segments have diminishing operational value as they age. Implement S3 lifecycle policies that transition advertising data to progressively lower-energy storage tiers as access frequency decreases. Delete expired campaign creatives, stale container images, and orphaned EBS volumes that consume storage energy without providing value. Retain bid event data for only as long as it serves operational, compliance, and analytics purposes.
+  **Establish an iterative process to review sustainability objectives:** Sustainability improvement is an iterative process, not a one-time effort. Establish a regular cadence to review sustainability KPIs (carbon per impression, resource utilization per campaign, cache hit rates, wasted compute from filtered bid requests). Use the AWS Sustainability service to measure carbon impact and set quarterly improvement targets. As advertising traffic patterns change and new AWS capabilities become available, continuously reassess whether the current architecture remains the most efficient option.
+  **Reduce network overhead by reusing connections and filtering low-value traffic:** Each new TLS connection for bid traffic adds handshake overhead that consumes energy. Persistent HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 connections amortize this overhead across many bid requests. Network-layer traffic filtering avoids compute energy for bid requests that would result in no-bid responses. If a workload consistently no-bids on more than 70% of requests from specific sources, filter that traffic before it reaches application compute. Energy consumed processing traffic that generates no advertising value is waste.
