# Reliability design principles
<a name="rel-design-principles"></a>

 These design principles help you achieve and maintain reliability for advertising workloads in the cloud. They extend the [lens-wide design principles](design-principles.md) with guidance specific to the three key reliability dimensions that define advertising systems: latency, uptime, and data security.
+  **Design for reliability across the three key dimensions of advertising:** Evaluate architectural approaches for building resilient systems through loosely coupled designs including SOA, microservices, and event-driven patterns. The three key dimensions that define reliability in the advertising industry are latency, uptime, and data security. While this is similar to the range of criticality in transactional systems found in the financial industry, there are unique expectations of resiliency for SSPs, exchanges, and DSPs.
+  **Define recovery objectives per component rather than per workload:** Avoid over- or under-architecting services by working backward from each service's recovery objectives, striking a balance with adjacent pillars such as cost optimization and operational excellence. Identify critical parts of the architecture and individually confirm their RPO and RTO. For example, with real-time bidding, delivery services have increased RPO and RTO requirements compared to creative services. Advertising customers accept ranges from milliseconds to hours as appropriate recovery, depending on the component.
+  **Architect for variable demand including the most challenging peak events:** Build a scalable architecture with automated scaling capacity to enable rapid increases and decreases of traffic gracefully. Know your historical load statistics and adjust load testing scenarios based on historical peaks to determine how the workload performs in unexpected situations and peak demand. Perform load tests at a minimum of five times the baseline of RTB traffic expectations to validate that allocated capacity meets low water mark, mean, and peak capacity projections.
+  **Implement chaos engineering to discover faults proactively:** Accept that everything fails, all the time, and safely disrupt things on your terms to discover faults and fragility so that you can later improve services. Advertising systems have components that are sensitive to disconnects, latency, and bandwidth changes. Use tools like AWS Fault Injection Service to inject failures that simulate network disruptions or resource unavailability. Based on results, update failure responses, monitoring, and alerting, then adapt runbooks and playbooks before practicing failure response with relevant teams.
+  **Adapt retry behavior to latency requirements:** Operating within 100 ms real-time bidding contracts, a single throttle and retry of five seconds can result in many failed bids and potentially insurmountable retry queues. Avoid this by adapting retries to fail fast on the bid path. Regulate request rates using algorithms such as token buckets, leaky buckets, or fixed window counters. Rate limiting can be an effective way to protect a service from overload, but it can reduce reliability if limits are set too low, because legitimate requests may be rejected or delayed.
+  **Maintain proportional scaling across all system components and external partners:** Providing reliability is paramount for advertising workloads, achievable by proportionally scaling all sub-components. When you integrate using a PrivateLink between DSP and SSP, your partner's requests may overwhelm your API frontend services, leading to throttling. In a microservices architecture, smaller services should drive larger capacity services, keeping them from being overwhelmed. The pub/sub pattern should be followed wherever possible to improve reliability through decoupled communication and load distribution.
+  **Design publisher-specific workloads for continuous operation:** FAST channels, SGAI pipelines, and Prebid Server implementations require uninterrupted reliability. An Availability Zone-level failure, a demand partner outage, or a content origin failure should not halt monetization. Design each publisher workload layer with independent failure domains, automated failover, and graceful degradation, so that a failure in one component reduces yield rather than removing it.
