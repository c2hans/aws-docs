>
> **What THIS plan uniquely owns** — mechanisms that exist *only* for the **step** policy type (target tracking does not have them) and that no sibling models:
> 1. **The customer authors and wires the CloudWatch alarm themselves** (`put-metric-alarm --alarm-actions <PolicyARN>`), unlike target tracking where AWS auto-creates and manages the alarm pair. This opens the **alarm→policy trust chain** to caller control: `SetAlarmState` **force-triggers** the scaling action with no metric breach; `AlarmActions` ARN ownership is **not documented as validated**; `DisableAlarmActions`/`DeleteAlarms` cannot be IAM-scoped by *what* they are wired to (SS1 — the headline).
> 2. **`ExactCapacity` ("Set to") adjustment type** — a direct capacity-SET primitive (set-to-0 → whole-fleet terminate; set-to-max → cost) that target tracking has no equivalent of (SS2).
> 3. **Caller-defined `StepAdjustments` with a null-bounded (±∞) open-ended top step**, and **no documented numeric cap on `ScalingAdjustment` / `MinAdjustmentMagnitude`** (SS3).
> 4. **Caller-settable `Cooldown` (default 300, no documented min/max) under the "continuously evaluates even while a scaling activity is in progress" rule** (SS4).
> 5. **Step + target-tracking mixing** that AWS documents as causing "undesirable behavior" — a step scale-in is *not* blocked when the TT policy is not ready → oscillation/flapping primitive (SS5).

## 1. Pentest Objectives
1. Show that a principal who can call `cloudwatch:SetAlarmState` on a step-scaling alarm can **force-fire the associated scaling policy on demand** — scaling (or terminating, via SS2) a Spot Fleet with **no real metric breach, no threshold, no evaluation period** — and that IAM cannot restrict this by *what the alarm is wired to* (only `aws:ResourceTag` scopes it) (SS1).
2. Show that `cloudwatch:PutMetricAlarm` accepts an `AlarmActions` scaling-policy ARN **without a documented ownership check**, so an intra-account principal (holding the `Resource:"*"` sample policy) can **wire a new alarm to another team's fleet scaling policy ARN** (a known/derivable string) and drive that fleet's capacity — an intra-account confused-deputy (SS1).
3. Show that a step policy with adjustment type **`ExactCapacity` set to `0`** scales the fleet to **0 → terminates every instance** via the two SLR hops, reachable by a caller holding **no `ec2:TerminateInstances` and no `ec2:ModifySpotFleetRequest`** — and that `ExactCapacity` to a high value forces maximal cost (SS2).
4. Determine whether a single **open-ended top `StepAdjustment` (null upper bound = +∞) with a large `PercentChangeInCapacity`/absolute `ScalingAdjustment`** vaults the fleet to `MaxCapacity` on one breach, and whether `ScalingAdjustment`/`MinAdjustmentMagnitude` are numerically unbounded (SS3).
5. Determine whether a **`Cooldown` of `0`/very small** combined with "continuously evaluates while a scaling activity is in progress" produces runaway over-scaling, and whether a **huge scale-in `Cooldown`** freezes scale-in (SS4).
6. Confirm whether **mixing a step policy with a target-tracking policy** on the same fleet lets a step scale-in run that the TT policy was not ready for, producing owner-adverse oscillation (SS5).
7. Determine whether `DisableAlarmActions`/`DeleteAlarms` on a step-scaling alarm silently disables scaling while `describe-scaling-policies` still reports the policy present (SS1 monitoring-integrity shape; parallels TT2 but on a *customer-owned* alarm).

## 2. Components, Assets, and Design

**Mechanism (not marketing).** Step scaling is one of three Spot Fleet automatic-scaling policy types. Its defining difference from target tracking: **"With step scaling policies, you create and manage the CloudWatch alarms that invoke the scaling process. When an alarm is breached, Application Auto Scaling initiates the scaling policy associated with that alarm."** (`application-auto-scaling-step-scaling-policies.html`). Target tracking auto-creates its alarms; step scaling does **not** — the customer runs `put-metric-alarm` and sets `--alarm-actions <PolicyARN>` to the ARN returned by `put-scaling-policy` (confirmed by `create-step-scaling-policy-cli.html` Step 3, title "Create an alarm that **invokes** a scaling policy"). `PutScalingPolicy`'s response `Alarms` array is populated only for target tracking ("The CloudWatch alarms created for the target tracking scaling policy"); for step scaling it is empty and the caller owns the wiring. **This exposes the alarm→policy trust chain to the caller, which is the net-new attack surface of this page.**

- **Customer-facing interface:** SigV4 IAM. Console ("Spot Requests → Auto Scaling tab → Configure → Step scaling policy", default policies `ScaleUp`/`ScaleDown`) or CLI/API: `application-autoscaling register-scalable-target` → `application-autoscaling put-scaling-policy --policy-type StepScaling --step-scaling-policy-configuration <json>` → `cloudwatch put-metric-alarm --alarm-actions <PolicyARN>`.
- **The scalable resource / addressing:** a Spot Fleet request, `sfr-<UUID>`, addressed as the AAS 3-tuple `ServiceNamespace=ec2`, `ResourceId=spot-fleet-request/sfr-xxxx`, `ScalableDimension=ec2:spot-fleet-request:TargetCapacity` — all caller-supplied strings. Scalable-target ARN: `arn:aws:application-autoscaling:<region>:<acct>:scalable-target/<opaque-id>`. Scaling-policy ARN (the alarm-action target): `arn:aws:autoscaling:<region>:<acct>:scalingPolicy:<uuid>:resource/ec2/spot-fleet-request/sfr-xxxx:policyName/<name>` — **derivable from the fleet id + policy name an intra-account party can read** (SS1).
- **The policy object (`StepScalingPolicyConfiguration`)** — the new attack surface (verbatim from `API_StepScalingPolicyConfiguration.md`):
  - `AdjustmentType` (String; valid `ChangeInCapacity | PercentChangeInCapacity | ExactCapacity`; field metadata says **Required: No** but prose says "required if you are adding a new step scaling policy configuration" — a doc self-contradiction, see SS6). **`ExactCapacity` is the set-to primitive** (SS2).
  - `StepAdjustments` (Array of `StepAdjustment`; "At least one … is required if you are adding a new … configuration" vs metadata Required: No). Each `StepAdjustment`: `ScalingAdjustment` (Integer, **Required: Yes**; "For exact capacity, you must specify a non-negative value"); `MetricIntervalLowerBound` (Double; "A null value indicates negative infinity"); `MetricIntervalUpperBound` (Double; "A null value indicates positive infinity"; "must be greater than the lower bound"). Rules: no overlap/gap; at most one null lower bound; at most one null upper bound; both can't be null in one step. **No numeric cap on `ScalingAdjustment`** (SS3).
  - `MinAdjustmentMagnitude` (Integer; "The minimum value to scale by when the adjustment type is `PercentChangeInCapacity`." No documented range) (SS3).
  - `Cooldown` (Integer seconds; "If not specified, the default value is 300." No documented min/max) (SS4).
  - `MetricAggregationType` (String; `Average | Minimum | Maximum`; null → `Average`) (SS4/SS5).
- **The alarm (caller-authored):** `put-metric-alarm` with caller-chosen `--metric-name`/`--namespace` (AWS metric **or own custom metric**), `--threshold`, `--comparison-operator`, `--evaluation-periods`, `--period`, and `--alarm-actions <PolicyARN>`. `AlarmActions` is "Array of strings … Maximum number of 5 items", accepts an Autoscaling `scalingPolicy` ARN; **no documented ownership validation** (SS1).
- **Processes / identities:**
  - *Application Auto Scaling control loop* — SLR **`AWSServiceRoleForApplicationAutoScaling_EC2SpotFleetRequest`**, managed policy **`AWSApplicationAutoscalingEC2SpotFleetRequestPolicy`** = `ec2:DescribeSpotFleetRequests`, `ec2:ModifySpotFleetRequest`, `cloudwatch:PutMetricAlarm`, `cloudwatch:DescribeAlarms`, `cloudwatch:DeleteAlarms` — **all `Resource:"*"`, no Condition** (auto-scaling Area S1). Note the SLR does **not** hold `SetAlarmState`, `DisableAlarmActions`, or `application-autoscaling:PutScalingPolicy` — those are purely customer-side (so SS1's force-trigger/silence are *customer-identity* actions, not SLR actions).
  - *EC2 Spot Fleet* — scale-out via the Spot Fleet SLR (identity-policy `RunInstances` conditions do NOT propagate — `create-spot-fleet` plan); scale-in terminates ("When a Spot Fleet terminates a Spot Instance because the target capacity was decreased, the instance receives a Spot Instance interruption notice").
- **Untrusted-data entry points unique to this page:** the **caller-authored alarm and its `AlarmActions` wiring**; **`SetAlarmState`**; the **`ExactCapacity` value**; the **open-ended step bounds / adjustment magnitudes**; the **`Cooldown`**; the **metric/aggregation selection** in the alarm.
- **KMS / secrets:** none generated by this surface (null — §8).


## 3. Trust-Boundary Map

| From (actor / zone) | To (resource / zone) | Crossing mechanism | **Breach oracle** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal with `cloudwatch:SetAlarmState` on a step alarm | Another workload's / the owner's fleet capacity | `SetAlarmState → ALARM` invokes `AlarmActions` (the scaling policy) by design; only `aws:ResourceTag` can scope the action | A scaling activity (incl. `ExactCapacity=0` terminate) fires with **no metric breach** → **metric-free on-demand capacity control / terminate** |
| Intra-account principal holding the `cloudwatch:*`-on-`"*"` sample policy | A fleet scaling policy ARN they do not own | `put-metric-alarm --alarm-actions <foreign PolicyARN>` with no documented ownership check | A new caller-owned alarm drives a *different* team's fleet capacity → **intra-account alarm-action confused-deputy** |
| Principal with `application-autoscaling:PutScalingPolicy` | The fleet's capacity envelope | `--policy-type StepScaling` with `ExactCapacity`/open-ended step | Fleet SET to an exact value (0 → all instances terminated; max → cost) via the SLR hops with no `ec2:*` on the caller |
| Principal with `cloudwatch:DisableAlarmActions`/`DeleteAlarms` on a step alarm | The fleet's scaling behavior + monitoring signal | Only `aws:ResourceTag` scopes these; no `AlarmActions`-aware gate | Scaling silently disabled while `describe-scaling-policies` reports the policy healthy → **monitoring-integrity break on a customer-owned alarm** |
| Step policy orchestration | A co-resident target-tracking policy on the same fleet | "step scale-in … will not be blocked" when TT not ready | Owner-adverse scale-in/scale-out oscillation the owner's TT policy keeps reversing |
| AAS control loop (SLR) | EC2 Spot Fleet capacity | SLR `ec2:ModifySpotFleetRequest` on `Resource:"*"`, no Condition | Capacity change on a fleet outside the scalable target the author legitimately registered (chains to auto-scaling A1/S1) |
| Any customer surface | **AWS's own service plane** | — | **HARD STOP** — any AWS-fleet identity/ARN/account = disclosure, preserve evidence |

## 5. Recommended Areas of Focus

### SS1 — Caller-owned alarm wiring: `SetAlarmState` force-trigger + unvalidated `AlarmActions` + un-scopable disable/delete (Lens CC / B / M / O / U) — PRIORITY (headline, step-scaling-only)
**Background.** Step scaling is the **only** policy type where the customer creates and owns the CloudWatch alarm and wires it to the policy: "With step scaling policies, you create and manage the CloudWatch alarms that invoke the scaling process" (`application-auto-scaling-step-scaling-policies.html`); the CLI tutorial's Step 3 is literally "Create an alarm that **invokes** a scaling policy" with `--alarm-actions <PolicyARN>` (`create-step-scaling-policy-cli.html`). Three documentation facts turn this ownership into attack surface:
1. **`SetAlarmState` is a documented force-trigger.** "Temporarily sets the state of an alarm for testing purposes. **When the updated state differs from the previous value, the action configured for the appropriate state is invoked.**" and "for EC2 Auto Scaling or application Auto Scaling alarm actions, the Auto Scaling policy uses the information in [`StateReasonData`] to take the correct action" (`API_SetAlarmState.md`). So a single `SetAlarmState --state-value ALARM` fires the scaling policy **with no real metric, no threshold, no evaluation period**.
2. **`SetAlarmState`/`DisableAlarmActions`/`DeleteAlarms` cannot be IAM-scoped by what the alarm triggers.** `list_cloudwatch` gives all three only the `alarm*` resource type and **only the `aws:ResourceTag/${TagKey}` condition key** — **none support `cloudwatch:AlarmActions`**. `cloudwatch:AlarmActions` is enforceable *only at `PutMetricAlarm` time*, and even then it filters by ARN *service prefix* ("The user can't create alarms that perform EC2 actions"), **not by account or owner** (`iam-cw-condition-keys-alarm-actions.md`).
3. **`PutMetricAlarm` `AlarmActions` has no documented ownership check.** The parameter is "Array of strings … Maximum number of 5 items", accepts an Autoscaling `scalingPolicy` ARN shape, and **nothing on the page states the embedded `account-id` must equal the caller's or that the target must exist / be owned by the caller** (`API_PutMetricAlarm.md`). The only account-crossing control documented is the unrelated cross-account *metrics* feature.

**Security Concern.** The `spot-fleet-auto-scaling-IAM.html` **sample identity policy grants `cloudwatch:PutMetricAlarm`, `DeleteAlarms`, `DisableAlarmActions`, `EnableAlarmActions` and `application-autoscaling:*` all on `Resource:"*"` with no `AlarmActions` condition** — an AWS-authored, copy-verbatim artifact (Lens R; audited in auto-scaling R1 — not re-audited here, used as the precondition). A principal holding it (a different team in the same account) can therefore, against a fleet it does not own:
- **(a) Force-fire** the victim's step policy on demand via `SetAlarmState` — combined with SS2 `ExactCapacity=0` this is a **metric-free, on-demand whole-fleet terminate** that needs neither `ec2:*` nor `application-autoscaling:PutScalingPolicy`, only `cloudwatch:SetAlarmState` on the alarm.
- **(b) Wire a new caller-owned alarm to the victim's `PolicyARN`** (derivable from the `sfr-` id + policy name, both readable intra-account via `DescribeScalingPolicies`, which has no resource/condition scoping) so an attacker-controlled metric/threshold drives the victim fleet — an **intra-account alarm-action confused-deputy**.
- **(c) `DisableAlarmActions`/`DeleteAlarms`** the victim's step alarm → scaling silently stops while `describe-scaling-policies` still shows the policy present and enabled (there is no `DisableScaleIn` flag on step policies, so the console shows a healthy policy — a **false signal**; parallels TT2 but on a *customer-owned* alarm the "do not edit the managed alarm" guarantee never applied to).

**High-level Test Scenarios.**
- **Claim:** `SetAlarmState --state-value ALARM` on a step-scaling alarm invokes the scaling policy with no metric breach. **Mechanism:** `API_SetAlarmState.md`. **Oracle:** on an authorized canary fleet with a step `ScaleUp`/`ScaleDown` policy, from a role holding **only** `cloudwatch:SetAlarmState` on the alarm (confirm via `SimulatePrincipalPolicy` it lacks `ec2:*`/`application-autoscaling:*`), call `SetAlarmState ALARM` with `StateReasonData`; observe a `describe-scaling-activities` entry and a `TargetCapacity` change. **Preconditions:** `cloudwatch:SetAlarmState` on the alarm. **Cost:** low. **Severity:** Medium–High intra-account (metric-free capacity control; High when chained to SS2 terminate).
- **Claim:** `PutMetricAlarm` accepts a foreign/arbitrary `scalingPolicy` ARN in `AlarmActions` with no ownership validation. **Oracle:** from a scoped role, create an alarm whose `--alarm-actions` is a *different* fleet's policy ARN; confirm the call succeeds and (ALARM) drives that fleet. If delivery is silently dropped, record as refuted/partial. **Severity:** High if it drives a non-owned fleet; else informational.
- **Claim:** `DisableAlarmActions`/`DeleteAlarms` on the step alarm halts scaling while the policy still reports healthy. **Oracle:** disable the alarm actions, drive the metric, observe no scaling activity yet `describe-scaling-policies` unchanged. **Severity:** Medium (availability/cost + false monitoring signal).
- **Claim (U/O):** no IAM mechanism can restrict `SetAlarmState`/`DisableAlarmActions` by the alarm's wired action. **Oracle:** diff `list_cloudwatch` condition keys for the three actions (only `aws:ResourceTag`) against the "do not disable" expectation — static, no touch. **Severity:** Informational→enabler.

**Doc evidence:** `application-auto-scaling-step-scaling-policies.html` (customer owns alarm); `create-step-scaling-policy-cli.html` (Step 3 `--alarm-actions <PolicyARN>`); `API_SetAlarmState.md` (invokes actions, AAS); `list_cloudwatch` (`SetAlarmState`/`DisableAlarmActions`/`DeleteAlarms` → `alarm*` + only `aws:ResourceTag`; `PutMetricAlarm` → `cloudwatch:AlarmActions`); `API_PutMetricAlarm.md` (no ownership check); `iam-cw-condition-keys-alarm-actions.html` (prefix-only scoping); `spot-fleet-auto-scaling-IAM.html` (sample `cloudwatch:*`/`application-autoscaling:*` on `"*"`). **Severity-if-true:** Medium–High (High at metric-free terminate via SS2).

### SS2 — `ExactCapacity` ("Set to") = direct capacity-SET primitive: set-to-0 terminate / set-to-max cost (Lens A / U / L) — PRIORITY (step-scaling-only)
**Background.** The target page offers "**Set to** – Set the target capacity of the fleet to the specified number of capacity units." The AAS overview defines the API type: "**ExactCapacity** — Change the current capacity of the scalable target to the specified value. **Specify a non-negative value** with this adjustment type." `ScalingAdjustment` is `Required: Yes`, Integer, and for exact capacity "must specify a non-negative value" (`API_StepAdjustment.md`). Target tracking has **no** `ExactCapacity` equivalent — it only targets a metric value — so this primitive is step-scaling-only.
**Security Concern.** `ExactCapacity = 0` is a **non-negative value**, i.e. allowed, and sets `TargetCapacity` to 0 → the AAS SLR calls `ec2:ModifySpotFleetRequest` to 0 → **every Spot Instance in the fleet is terminated** ("the instance receives a Spot Instance interruption notice"). The caller reaches this through `application-autoscaling:PutScalingPolicy` + an alarm trigger (or SS1 `SetAlarmState`), holding **no `ec2:TerminateInstances` and no `ec2:ModifySpotFleetRequest`** — a metric/step-driven variant of the terminate primitive in `modify-spot-fleet-attack-research-plan.md` and the scale-in-to-0 of TT3, but **more direct** (no custom-metric sculpting; a single `ExactCapacity:0` step + one ALARM). Conversely `ExactCapacity = MaxCapacity` pins the fleet at its ceiling (cost), chaining to auto-scaling U1 if `MaxCapacity` itself was set high. The only documented guard is `MinCapacity`/`MaxCapacity` clamping — "Scaling policies do not scale your fleet below the minimum capacity or above the maximum capacity" (target page) — so set-to-0 requires `MinCapacity=0` (the Spot Fleet default/common case; confirm).
**High-level Test Scenarios.**
- **Claim:** a step policy with one step `{MetricIntervalLowerBound:0, ScalingAdjustment:0, AdjustmentType:ExactCapacity}` — i.e. ExactCapacity 0 — scales a `MinCapacity=0` fleet to 0 and terminates all instances when its alarm fires. **Oracle:** on a canary fleet (min 0), create the policy, trigger the alarm (SS1 `SetAlarmState` or a real breach), observe `FulfilledCapacity → 0` and instance termination; confirm the acting role lacks `ec2:TerminateInstances`/`ModifySpotFleetRequest` via `SimulatePrincipalPolicy`. **Preconditions:** `application-autoscaling:PutScalingPolicy` + alarm trigger; `MinCapacity=0`. **Cost:** low. **Severity:** High intra-account (whole-fleet destruction with no EC2 rights); **High** chained with SS1 (metric-free, on-demand).
- **Claim:** `ExactCapacity` is accepted at any non-negative value up to clamping, and `MinCapacity=0` is required for set-to-0. **Oracle:** attempt ExactCapacity 0 with `MinCapacity>0` (expect clamp to min) vs `MinCapacity=0` (expect 0); attempt a huge value (expect clamp to `MaxCapacity`). **Severity:** confirms the primitive bounds.
**Doc evidence:** `spot-fleet-step-scaling.html` ("Set to"); `step-scaling-policy-overview.html` (ExactCapacity, non-negative); `API_StepAdjustment.md` (`ScalingAdjustment` non-negative for exact); target page (min/max clamp, interruption notice). **Severity-if-true:** High (full-fleet terminate) / Medium (cost pin).

### SS3 — Caller-defined open-ended steps + uncapped `ScalingAdjustment`/`MinAdjustmentMagnitude` = one-breach cost vault (Lens L / U)
**Background.** `StepAdjustments` are entirely caller-defined. A step with a **null `MetricIntervalUpperBound` = positive infinity** is an open-ended top step: any breach past its lower bound, however small, triggers its full adjustment. `ScalingAdjustment` (Integer) and `MinAdjustmentMagnitude` (Integer) have **no documented numeric cap** (`API_StepScalingPolicyConfiguration.md`, `API_StepAdjustment.md`). The only validation the docs state is structural: no overlap/gap, at most one null lower and one null upper bound, both-null forbidden, upper>lower.
**Security Concern.** A policy with a single open-ended top step `{lower:0, upper:null, ScalingAdjustment: <large>, AdjustmentType: PercentChangeInCapacity|ChangeInCapacity}` makes **one alarm breach of any magnitude request maximal capacity** — "add as fast as it can" with no per-step ceiling other than `MaxCapacity`. `MinAdjustmentMagnitude` compounds `PercentChangeInCapacity`: a large magnitude forces a big absolute add even on a small fleet ("you specified a minimum increment of 2 … adds 2"). Combined with SS4 (cooldown=0, continuous evaluation) and SS1 (force-trigger), this is a monotonic cost amplifier the fleet owner cannot bound below `MaxCapacity`. Unlike target tracking, there is no proportional "scale-in only >10% below target" softening — step scale-in requires a *separate* alarm/policy the attacker simply omits.
**High-level Test Scenarios.**
- **Claim:** a single open-ended top step with a large percent/absolute adjustment vaults the fleet to `MaxCapacity` on one breach. **Oracle:** on a canary fleet, create such a policy; trigger once; observe `FulfilledCapacity` jump to the ceiling in one activity. **Severity:** Medium (cost; bounded by `MaxCapacity` — High if `MaxCapacity` is high, chaining auto-scaling U1).
- **Claim:** `ScalingAdjustment`/`MinAdjustmentMagnitude` accept arbitrarily large integers with no server-side sanity bound. **Oracle:** `put-scaling-policy` with extreme values; record acceptance and the computed adjustment. **Severity:** confirms the amplifier.
**Doc evidence:** `step-scaling-policy-overview.html` (step adjustments, null bounds, PercentChangeInCapacity rounding, MinAdjustmentMagnitude example); `API_StepAdjustment.md` (null = ±infinity, no cap); `create-step-scaling-policy-cli.html` (open-ended third step). **Severity-if-true:** Medium (High with high `MaxCapacity`).

### SS4 — Caller-settable `Cooldown` + "continuously evaluates while scaling in progress" (Lens L / U)
**Background.** `Cooldown` is an optional Integer; "If not specified, the default value is 300." **No min/max documented** → `0` or a very large value is accepted. The overview states step scaling "will continue to respond to additional alarm breaches, **even while a scaling activity is in progress** … A cooldown period is used to protect against over-scaling due to multiple alarm breaches occurring in rapid succession." Scale-out cooldown counts in-flight capacity toward the next activity; scale-in activities are **blocked until the scale-in cooldown expires**, but "if another alarm triggers a scale-out … during the scale-in cooldown period, Application Auto Scaling scales out the target immediately … the scale-in cooldown period stops and doesn't complete."
**Security Concern.** Two owner-adverse settings: (a) **`Cooldown=0`/tiny on a scale-out policy** removes the over-scaling guard the docs say the cooldown exists to provide, so under continuous evaluation rapid repeated breaches each add capacity → faster runaway (compounds SS3). (b) **A very large scale-in `Cooldown`** blocks scale-in for that duration → the fleet cannot shrink (cost/availability-of-budget), while scale-out remains immediate (asymmetry favoring growth, and a scale-out mid-scale-in-cooldown aborts the pending scale-in). An attacker who authors (or via SS1 force-triggers) the policies chooses these knobs; the fleet owner cannot tune them away once set by a rogue co-author.
**High-level Test Scenarios.**
- **Claim:** `Cooldown:0` is accepted and yields rapid repeated scale-out under sustained breach. **Oracle:** create a scale-out step policy with `Cooldown:0`; hold the metric above threshold; count scaling activities per minute vs a `Cooldown:300` control. **Severity:** Medium (cost).
- **Claim:** a large scale-in `Cooldown` blocks scale-in while scale-out stays immediate. **Oracle:** set scale-in `Cooldown` high; trigger scale-in then scale-out; confirm scale-in blocked, scale-out immediate. **Severity:** Medium.
**Doc evidence:** `step-scaling-policy-overview.html` (Cooldown section, default 300, continuous evaluation, scale-in blocked / scale-out interrupts); `API_StepScalingPolicyConfiguration.md` (`Cooldown` no min/max). **Severity-if-true:** Medium (cost/availability-of-budget).

### SS5 — Step + target-tracking mixing = owner-adverse oscillation; largest-capacity-wins (Lens L / A / U)
**Background.** `PutScalingPolicy` and `target-tracking-scaling-policy-overview.html` both warn (verbatim, identical): "We recommend caution … when using target tracking scaling policies with step scaling policies because conflicts between these policies can cause undesirable behavior. For example, **if the step scaling policy initiates a scale-in activity before the target tracking policy is ready to scale in, the scale-in activity will not be blocked.** After the scale-in activity completes, the target tracking policy could instruct the scalable target to scale out again." The general composition rule applies: "Application Auto Scaling gives precedence to the policy that provides the **largest capacity** for both scale out and scale in" (worked example: 3 vs 200% of 10 → scales to 30).
**Security Concern.** Where a fleet owner uses a target-tracking policy (the AWS-recommended steady-state choice), a second principal who can `PutScalingPolicy` adds a **step** policy whose scale-in "will not be blocked" by the TT policy's readiness logic → forces a scale-in the owner's TT policy immediately reverses with a scale-out → **sustained oscillation/flapping** (instance churn, repeated Spot interruption notices, cost from launch/terminate cycling) that neither policy alone would cause. Conversely a step policy with a large open-ended scale-out (SS3) wins the "largest-capacity" arbitration over the owner's conservative TT target. This is distinct from TT1 (which is multi-*TT*-policy "any-out/all-in"); the step/TT mix is the step-scaling-specific conflict AWS explicitly documents as "undesirable."
**High-level Test Scenarios.**
- **Claim:** adding a step scale-in policy to a TT-managed fleet produces oscillation the owner's TT policy keeps reversing. **Oracle:** on a canary fleet with an owner TT policy, add a step scale-in policy and trigger it; observe scale-in then TT scale-out cycling in `describe-scaling-activities`. **Severity:** Medium–High intra-account (churn/cost; owner cannot stop it without finding/deleting the rogue step policy).
- **Claim:** a step open-ended scale-out wins largest-capacity arbitration over the owner's TT target. **Oracle:** co-locate; drive both; confirm the step figure wins. **Severity:** feeds SS3.
**Doc evidence:** `API_PutScalingPolicy.md` + `target-tracking-scaling-policy-overview.html` (mixing-caution + largest-capacity rule). **Severity-if-true:** Medium–High.

### SS6 — `maintain`-type prerequisite + `AdjustmentType`/`StepAdjustments` "Required: No" vs prose-required (Lens P / U)
**Background.** Prerequisite (target page): "The Spot Fleet request must have a request type of `maintain`. Automatic scaling is not supported for requests of type `request`." Separately, `API_StepScalingPolicyConfiguration.md` lists `AdjustmentType` and `StepAdjustments` as **field-metadata `Required: No`** while the prose says each is "required if you are adding a new step scaling policy configuration" — a documented self-contradiction.
**Security Concern.** (a) Is the `maintain`-only precondition enforced at `RegisterScalableTarget`/`PutScalingPolicy` time, or does registration succeed and silently no-op against a `request`-type fleet (consistent with the AAS "if the specified resource is not active, this operation does not change the resource's current capacity" deferred-activation behavior — auto-scaling A1)? A fail-open yields a dormant, posture-confusing policy. (b) Does the API accept a step policy with **no `StepAdjustments` / no `AdjustmentType`** (per the permissive field metadata)? If so, what is the scaling behavior of an "empty" step policy wired to an alarm — a fail-open or a no-op? Low direct impact but a doc-vs-enforcement integrity question worth a cheap confirm.
**High-level Test Scenarios.**
- **Claim:** a step policy is accepted against a `request`-type fleet and silently never scales. **Oracle:** register a `request`-type fleet, `put-scaling-policy StepScaling`, observe accept + no scaling. **Severity:** Low (posture confusion).
- **Claim:** `put-scaling-policy` accepts a `StepScaling` config missing `AdjustmentType`/`StepAdjustments`. **Oracle:** submit a minimal/empty config; record whether rejected (prose) or accepted (metadata). **Severity:** Low–Informational (doc-vs-API integrity).
**Doc evidence:** `spot-fleet-step-scaling.html` (maintain-only prereq); `API_StepScalingPolicyConfiguration.md` (Required-field vs prose contradiction). **Severity-if-true:** Low.

## 8. Null hypotheses / doc gaps
- **Lens A (cross-account / cross-tenant):** null for this surface — the step policy, the scalable target (`scalable-target/<id>` ARN in the *caller's* account), and the caller-authored alarm are all account-scoped; `sfr-<UUID>` is 128-bit. Pages checked: `spot-fleet-step-scaling.html`, `step-scaling-policy-overview.html`, `create-step-scaling-policy-cli.html`, `list_application-autoscaling`, `list_cloudwatch`. In-scope realization is intra-account cross-team/cross-workload (SS1/SS2/SS5), reached via auto-scaling A1.
- **`AlarmActions` cross-account delivery (gates SS1-(b) cross-account severity):** **doc-gap, not a confirmed null.** `API_PutMetricAlarm.md` documents no ownership validation of the action ARN, but also documents **no cross-account delivery mechanism** for an Autoscaling `scalingPolicy` ARN (unlike SNS/Lambda which have resource policies). Whether CloudWatch silently drops a cross-account `scalingPolicy` action vs. accepts-but-fails-at-delivery is undocumented — confirm empirically before assigning any cross-account severity; default assumption is intra-account only.
- **Lens H (KMS / encryption context):** null — step policy, alarm, and metric spec generate/store no key, secret, or encryption context. Pages checked: all step pages + `API_PutScalingPolicy.md`, `API_PutMetricAlarm.md`.
- **Lens G (SSRF):** null — no field the step-scaling control plane dereferences/fetches server-side. The alarm's `Namespace`/`MetricName`/`Dimensions` are matched against CloudWatch metadata, not fetched as URLs. Pages checked: `API_PutMetricAlarm.md`, `step-scaling-policy-overview.html`.
- **Lens Q (upload) / F (translation injection):** null — no file upload, no query/wire-protocol translation.
- **Lens V/D (network segmentation / data→control):** null for the scaling control loop (control-plane-to-control-plane API traffic, no customer-reachable ENI). The data-plane angle lives on the launched instances (out of scope here).
- **`MinCapacity=0` prevalence (gates SS2 set-to-0 terminate):** confirm whether the canary/target fleet actually permits `MinCapacity=0` — set-to-0 requires it (the min/max clamp otherwise floors the fleet at the min). The target page states the clamp but not the default; treat SS2 full-terminate as conditional on `MinCapacity=0`.
- **`maintain`-type precondition + Required-field-vs-prose enforcement (SS6):** **doc-gap** — the docs state the `maintain` prerequisite and list `AdjustmentType`/`StepAdjustments` as both "Required: No" (metadata) and required (prose); neither the enforcement point nor the empty-config behavior is documented. Confirm before relying on either reading.
- **`ScalingAdjustment`/`MinAdjustmentMagnitude`/`Cooldown` numeric bounds (SS3/SS4):** the API reference states no min/max for any of them — treat "unbounded" as a documentation fact to confirm against the live `put-scaling-policy` validator (a server-side sanity bound may exist but is undocumented).
- **Confirmed Spot Fleet CloudWatch metric strings (reused from the TT plan):** namespace `AWS/EC2Spot`, dimension `FleetRequestId`, metrics `CPUUtilization`/`NetworkIn`/`NetworkOut`; `TargetCapacity`/`FulfilledCapacity` also exposed (console "EC2 Spot → Fleet Request Metrics"). The step alarm may use any of these or a fully custom metric.
- **Online-vs-offline doc drift (provenance, Informational):** the online `list_cloudwatch` now lists an additional condition key `cloudwatch:AlarmTagsPropagationEnabled` on `PutMetricAlarm`/`PutCompositeAlarm`/`PutMetricAlarmBasedOnLogsAlarm` that is absent from the offline mirror snapshot. It governs tag propagation into notification payloads — **unrelated to action-ARN ownership/scoping**, so it does not change any SS1 conclusion, but is a confirmed mirror staleness item (reinforces the memory rule to always cross-check online).
- **Doc self-contradiction (Informational, file-worthy):** `API_StepScalingPolicyConfiguration.md` marks `AdjustmentType` and `StepAdjustments` as field-metadata `Required: No` while the prose declares both required when adding a new config (SS6).
